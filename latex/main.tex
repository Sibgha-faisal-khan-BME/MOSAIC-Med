\begin{abstract}
Deep neural networks for physiological signal classification predominantly rely on monolithic parameter scaling, which incurs high computational latency and uncalibrated overconfidence under simulated signal stress. In resource-constrained 12-lead electrocardiogram (ECG) multi-label diagnosis across 21,799 PTB-XL records, this study investigates distributing an invariant $\sim$2.0M parameter budget among compact specialized networks (MOSAIC-Med). The multiplicity hypothesis (H1) is validated: split architectures ($K=2$: AUROC $0.9085 \pm 0.0007$; $K=4$: $0.9084 \pm 0.0008$) statistically surpass the monolithic baseline ($K=1$: $0.9059 \pm 0.0010$, DeLong $p=3.63 \times 10^{-4}$). However, extreme over-fragmentation at $K=16$ degrades AUROC to $0.9048$ and triples latency. Architectural diversity (H2) yields a consistent descriptive improvement trend ($0.9110 \pm 0.0006$; Holm $p=0.058$) with lower CKA similarity ($0.9008$ vs $0.9458$), but is not statistically significant at $\alpha=0.05$. Learned Top-2 sparse routing (H3) achieves competitive discrimination ($0.9113$) while reducing computation by $28.2\%$ relative to dense ensembling (297.8 vs 414.9 MFLOPs). Selective abstention (H4) on the $10\%$ most uncertain records reduces multi-label Hamming risk among retained records by $4.18\%$ ($p=0.0093$). Factorial ablation confirms diversity regularization prevents router collapse ($0.9088$ vs $0.9049$).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

In clinical artificial intelligence, foundational research established deep ensembling as an effective paradigm for reliable predictive uncertainty estimation~\cite{lakshminarayanan2017simple}. Parallel foundational developments in safety-critical classification introduced selective prediction frameworks with integrated reject options, formalizing mechanisms for deep neural networks to abstain on ambiguous instances~\cite{geifman2019selectivenet}. At the same time, representational similarity metrics like Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) facilitated the quantitative assessment of hidden layer representations across the different families of neural networks~\cite{kornblith2019similarity}. The availability of large clinical repositories like PTB-XL~\cite{wagner2020ptbxl} spurred systematic benchmarking of typical 12-lead electrocardiography (ECG) across deep architectures in cardiovascular medicine~\cite{strodthoff2021deep}.

Despite these groundworks, key challenges related to the deployment of real-world clinical AI were brought to the fore in the literature during 2024. Black et al.~\cite{black2024multiplicity} showed predictive multiplicity in clinical machine learning models: that several networks with the same overall discrimination exhibit conflicting predictions for high-risk patients. Forestier et al.~\cite{forestier2024ortho} showed that feature orthogonality and the negative correlation between sub-networks improves generalizability in time-series ensembles to avoid correlated failure modes. For hospital deployment workflows, Kompa et al.~\cite{kompa2024second} developed second opinion workflows, which showed that uncertainty-directed triage to human cardiologists significantly reduces diagnostic risk. However, monolithic scaling continued to be the main paradigm, including foundation models, like the ECGFounder which is pre-trained on 10 million recordings~\cite{li2024ecgfounder}. Scale is, however, costly in terms of computational overheads or latency in the case of edge hardware, thus prompting research into split learning and parameter partitioning for decentralized clinical edge inferences~\cite{zhang2024split}.

In 2025, investigations went into resource allocation, diversity formulations, and clinical escalation protocols. Argha et al.~\cite{argha2025selective} proposed selective classification and dynamic clinician escalation for 12-lead ECG which was successfully validated, where they showed that avoiding the action of `abstain' on borderline tracings avoids catastrophic false positives. To tackle the extreme compute demands, Chen et al.~\cite{chen2025budget} proposed parameter budget allocation and ensemble pruning, demonstrating that the unconstrained model stacking approach is impractical for bedside and wearable monitors. A method to mitigate rigid foundation footprints is to use EnECG~\cite{xu2025enecg} that integrates foundation backbone, parameter-efficient adapters, and learned gating. At the same time, theoretical studies by Zhou et al.~\cite{zhou2025diversity} derived diversity decompositions for collaborative ensembles, demonstrating sub-additive error scaling of combining different kinds of inductive biases. In the context of realistic sensor distortion, the 2026 frontier in physiological time-series AI has landed on adaptive routing and mixture-of-experts (MoE) formulations. Han et al.~\cite{han2026cogmoe} proposed CogMoE, which showed the maintenance of diagnostic fidelity in the presence of acute physiological noise, by enabling quality-aware gating. Mert et al.~\cite{mert2026multilabel} compared five deep architectures through the analysis of a comprehensive PTB-XL benchmark, and found that heterogeneous ensembles consistently outperform the individual foundation networks. At the same time, Wang et al.~\cite{wang2026domain} introduced domain-expert-guided routing to handle the sensor lead dropouts; Xu et al.~\cite{xu2026ecgmoe} presented a new technique named ECG-MoE, separating morphology features from rhythm periodicity.

However, while these are noteworthy progress, current medical MoE and ensemble methods have two major shortcomings: they increase the capacity of the aggregate parameters without considering multi-model multiplicity under a tightly constrained parameter budget ceiling, and they mostly replicate homogeneous backbones without considering orthogonal inductive biases~\cite{zhou2025diversity}.

In this study, we impose the invariant $\sim$2.0M parameter budget ceiling, and test if the small-model multiplicity can outperform the scale of monolithic models.

There are four contributions:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*), leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Fixed-Budget Multiplicity}: where $K \in \{1, 2, 4, 8, 16\}$ experts with an invariant budget of 2.0M to prove Capacity over-fragmentation boundary (validating Hypothesis~1: Multiplicity Superiority).
\item \textbf{Inductive Bias Diversity}: A parameter-matched comparison (+0.92\%) between homogeneous (4$\times$ResNet) and heterogeneous (ResNet, MultiScale, TCN, Attention) experts quantified with linear CKA~\cite{kornblith2019similarity}, which shows a consistent improvement trend (adjusted p = 0.058) for Hypothesis~2: Diversity Benefit.
\item \textbf{Sparse Adaptive Routing}: A lightweight patient-specific router regularized with the diversity entropy and failure penalties, with dynamic Top-2 routing and reduced inference FLOPs by 28.2\% (empowering Hypothesis~3: Routing Efficiency).
\item \textbf{Stress Robustness \& Uncertainty Deferral}: Systematic evaluation across five simulated stress tests and internal site domain shift, coupled with an uncertainty-triggered deferral protocol yielding monotonic retained risk reduction~\cite{kompa2024second, argha2025selective} (validating Hypothesis~4: Risk Reduction).
\end{enumerate}

As documented in Table~\ref{tab:data_summary} and illustrated in Boxes 1--5 of Fig.~\ref{fig:method_flowchart}, records are partitioned using the official patient-stratified 10-fold split: Folds 1--8 for training, Fold 9 for validation, and Fold 10 strictly held out for final testing, guaranteeing zero patient identity leakage~\cite{strodthoff2021deep}. Diagnostic classification is framed as multi-label binary prediction across five clinical superclasses: Normal ECG (NORM), Myocardial Infarction (MI), ST/T Changes (STTC), Conduction Defect (CD), and Hypertrophy (HYP). All signals are standardized instance-by-instance across time ($L=1000$) independently per lead: $x_c'(t) = (x_c(t) - \mu_c) / \max(\sigma_c, 10^{-6})$, with extreme values clipped to $[-10.0, 10.0]$. Because normalization statistics are computed strictly on individual 10-second recordings, zero dataset-level training distribution statistics are used during inference.

\subsection{Multiplicity and Architectural Diversity}
\label{subsec:multiplicity_diversity}

In standard monolithic deep learning, a single neural network $f_\Theta(x)$ maps $x \to \hat{y} \in [0, 1]^M$, subject to $|\Theta| \le B$. In the proposed multiplicity framework (Box 6 of Fig.~\ref{fig:method_flowchart}), this fixed capacity quota $B \approx 2.0 \times 10^6$ parameters is partitioned among $K$ distinct sub-networks $\{f_{\theta_k}\}_{k=1}^K$ such that:
\begin{equation}
\sum_{k=1}^K |\theta_k| \le B \approx 2.0 \times 10^6 \quad \text{parameters}.
\label{eq:budget_constraint}
\end{equation}

For homogeneous capacity scaling, each sub-network $f_{\theta_k}$ is instantiated as a 1D Residual Network (ResNet-1D) with target capacity $|\theta_k| \approx \frac{B}{K}$. Base convolution filter widths $W_K$ enforce the nominal $2.0 \times 10^6$ budget within a formal $\pm 3.0\%$ tolerance envelope ($[1.94\text{M}, 2.06\text{M}]$): $K=1$ ($W_1=65$, 2,019,945 params, $+1.00\%$), $K=2$ ($W_2=46$, 2,030,450 params, $+1.52\%$), $K=4$ ($W_4=32$, 1,975,572 params, $-1.22\%$), $K=8$ ($W_8=23$, 2,054,952 params, $+2.75\%$), and $K=16$ ($W_{16}=16$, 2,009,680 params, $+0.48\%$).

To evaluate whether combining orthogonal inductive biases surpasses homogeneous replication, a 4-expert parameter-matched heterogeneous arm (Boxes 7--8 in Fig.~\ref{fig:method_flowchart}) ensembles four distinct paradigms calibrated to $\sim 500\text{K}$ parameters: (i)~\textbf{ResNet-1D} (493,893 params, 3 residual stages, kernels 7, 5, 3); (ii)~\textbf{MultiScale-CNN} (508,909 params, parallel kernels 3, 7, 15, 31); (iii)~\textbf{TCN-1D} (490,661 params, dilated causal convolutions $d \in \{1, 2, 4, 8\}$); and (iv)~\textbf{ConvAttention-1D} (500,225 params, conv stem downsampling $4\times$, 2-layer Transformer, $d_{\text{model}}=140$, 4 heads). The aggregate capacity is $1,993,688$ parameters, matching the homogeneous 4xResNet arm ($1,975,572$) within a $+0.92\%$ margin.

To empirically verify representational diversity, Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA)~\cite{kornblith2019similarity} is evaluated on penultimate feature activations over the held-out test cohort ($N = 2,198$). For centered activation matrices $X \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times D_1}, Y \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times D_2}$, linear CKA is:
\begin{equation}
\text{CKA}(X, Y) = \frac{\|Y^T X\|_F^2}{\|X^T X\|_F \|Y^T Y\|_F}.
\label{eq:cka}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Dynamic Gating and Routing Optimization}
\label{subsec:routing}

To dynamically dispatch experts without evaluating every waveform (Boxes 9--10 in Fig.~\ref{fig:method_flowchart}), a lightweight Adaptive Router $g_\phi(x)$ ($16,132$ parameters, $1.19$ MFLOPs) maps 12-lead inputs to patient-specific gating weights $w(x) \in \Delta^{K-1}$:
\begin{equation}
w(x) = \text{Softmax}\left(W_2 \cdot \text{ReLU}(W_1 \cdot \text{Pool}(\text{Conv1D}(x)))\right).
\label{eq:gating_softmax}
\end{equation}
The router brings the total routed heterogeneous architecture to $2,009,820$ parameters ($1,993,688 + 16,132$), which remains strictly within a $+0.49\%$ margin of the nominal 2.0M budget. In sparse Top-2 inference, gating weights are renormalized over the two highest-scoring experts:
\begin{equation}
\tilde{w}_k(x) = \begin{cases} 
\frac{w_k(x)}{\sum_{j \in \text{Top2}(w(x))} w_j(x)} & \text{if } k \in \text{Top2}(w(x)), \\
0 & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:top2_sparsity}
\end{equation}
Predictions are $\hat{z}(x) = \sum_{k=1}^K \tilde{w}_k(x) z_k(x)$, and $\hat{y}_m(x) = \sigma(\hat{z}_m(x))$. The router optimizes composite loss (Box 11 of Fig.~\ref{fig:method_flowchart}):
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{diag}} + \lambda_{\text{div}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{div}} + \lambda_{\text{fail}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{fail}},
\label{eq:composite_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_{\text{diag}}$ is BCE, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{div}} = -\mathcal{H}(\bar{w})$ prevents expert collapse by maximizing mean gating entropy, and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{fail}} = \sum_k w_k(x) \mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE}}(f_{\theta_k}(x), y)$ penalizes routing toward inaccurate sub-networks ($\lambda_{\text{div}} = 0.05, \lambda_{\text{fail}} = 0.10$).

\subsection{Uncertainty Estimation and Selective Classification}
\label{subsec:uncertainty}

In safety-critical workflows (Boxes 12--15 of Fig.~\ref{fig:method_flowchart}), automated models should signal low confidence on ambiguous tracings to facilitate triage or clinician review~\cite{kompa2024second}. Predictive uncertainty $U(x)$ combines binary predictive entropy and epistemic ensemble disagreement~\cite{lakshminarayanan2017simple}:
\begin{equation}
U(x) = \sum_{m=1}^M \mathcal{H}(\hat{y}_m(x)) + \alpha \cdot \sum_{m=1}^M \frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^K (\sigma(z_{k, m}(x)) - \hat{y}_m(x))^2,
\label{eq:uncertainty}
\end{equation}
where the first term represents multi-label binary predictive entropy (aleatoric uncertainty), and the second term measures ensemble variance across expert predictions (epistemic disagreement). The scaling coefficient $\alpha = 2.0$ was selected on Validation Fold 9 to minimize the Area Under the Risk-Coverage Curve (AURC), and deferral thresholds for coverage levels $c \in \{0.80, 0.85, 0.90, 0.95\}$ were fitted on Fold 9 quantiles before applying to held-out test data.
Under selective classification with coverage $c \in (0, 1]$, the model classifies the fraction $c$ with lowest uncertainty $U(x)$ and abstains on the remaining $1-c$ fraction~\cite{geifman2019selectivenet}. Diagnostic error on the retained cohort $\mathcal{S}_c$ is measured via multi-label Hamming risk:
\begin{equation}
\text{Risk}(c) = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}_c| \cdot M} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{S}_c} \sum_{m=1}^M \mathbb{I}\left(\mathbb{I}(\hat{y}_{i, m} \ge 0.5) \ne y_{i, m}\right).
\label{eq:selective_risk}
\end{equation}
Relative risk reduction at coverage $c$ is calculated per-seed as $\text{RR}_s(c) = (\text{Risk}_{\text{Single}, s}(c) - \text{Risk}_{\text{MOSAIC}, s}(c)) / \text{Risk}_{\text{Single}, s}(c)$ before taking the mean across the five random seeds ($4.18\% \pm 2.02\%$ at $c=90\%$), preserving paired within-seed evaluation rather than dividing rounded aggregate cohort means.

\section{Experimental Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Fixed-Budget Multiplicity Scaling ($K=1$ to $K=16$)}
\label{subsec:res_scaling}

Table~\ref{tab:scaling_results} and Fig.~\ref{fig:scaling_tradeoff} summarize empirical scaling trade-offs as the invariant 2.0M parameter budget is partitioned across $K \in \{1, 2, 4, 8, 16\}$ experts over five independent seeds (42--46).

\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.92\columnwidth, height=4.6cm, keepaspectratio]{figures/scaling_tradeoff.png}

Solid curves show Macro AUROC (blue) and Macro AUPRC (teal) on the left axis; dashed curve shows probability calibration Brier score (purple, lower is better) on the right axis.}
\label{fig:scaling_tradeoff}

As revealed in Table~\ref{tab:scaling_results} and plotted in Fig.~\ref{fig:scaling_tradeoff}, partitioning the fixed 2.0M parameter quota into $K=2$ ($0.9085 \pm 0.0007$) and $K=4$ ($0.9084 \pm 0.0008$) sub-networks achieves statistically significant improvements over the monolithic $K=1$ architecture ($0.9059 \pm 0.0010$, DeLong test $p = 3.63 \times 10^{-4}$, Holm-adjusted $p = 1.09 \times 10^{-3}$). Pairwise discrimination tests on the held-out test cohort ($N=2,198$) evaluate paired DeLong tests across the $M=5$ diagnostic superclasses; variance and covariance of structural components are calculated per class, and $p$-values are combined across superclasses via Fisher's method. Multiple testing across the $K \in \{2, 4, 8, 16\}$ configurations vs. the monolithic baseline ($m=4$) is controlled using the Holm-Bonferroni procedure. Probability calibration follows an identical beneficial trend: Brier score drops from $0.0900$ for Single Large to $0.0881$ for $K=4$, representing a marked reduction in probability estimation error. 

The underlying mathematical mechanism driving this gain is ensemble variance reduction under an approximately fixed aggregate parameter budget: intermediate capacity splitting ($K=2, 4$) reduces idiosyncratic estimation error across diverse patient morphologies without reducing individual network capacity below the minimum threshold required to extract morphological 12-lead features. However, scaling to $K=16$ triggers an acute capacity over-fragmentation boundary. When the 2.0M budget is divided among 16 sub-networks, individual models receive only $\sim 125\text{K}$ parameters ($W_{16}=16$). At this impoverished capacity, individual networks lack the receptive field depth and spatial filter diversity needed to resolve subtle ST-elevation or multi-lead QRS changes. This results in the individual sub-networks' feature quality eroded and lowers the AUROC of the ensemble to $0.9048 \pm 0.0005$ (which falls below that of the monolithic baseline) and sequential latency of the ensemble is tripled to $6.78$~ms.

The physical cause of this performance disparity is representational diversity. The four homogeneous arms in the Replica Set consists of very similar replicas, as evidenced by the high feature redundancy between them, with an off-diagonal mean Linear CKA of 0.9458. All four sub-networks have identical receptive fields and residual connectivity, leading to nearly collinear feature subspaces, and correlated failure modes.

The heterogeneous arm on the other hand uses a different set of architectural inductive biases, reducing the mean pairwise CKA to 0.9008. MultiScale-CNN and ConvAttention-1D achieve the lowest representational similarity with each other (CKA = 0.8824). The functional orthogonality suggests the different types of feature extraction mechanisms: MultiScale-CNN: extracts localized multi-frequency wavelet features at parallel kernel sizes (3, 15, 31 samples); ConvAttention-1D: models long-range rhythm periodicity and cross-beat dependencies through global self-attention heads.

These non-redundant feature representations are combined by ensembling so that the heterogenous ensemble is able to reduce uncorrelated errors of individual networks to obtain more reliable diagnosis under an invariant parameter footprint.

The dense learned Adaptive Router (16K parameters) performs at peak discrimination ($0.9118 \pm 0.0004$, Holm $p = 5.27 \times 10^{-5}$) at 414.9 MFLOPs. The Sparse Top-2 Router achieves empirical discrimination of $0.9113 \pm 0.0008$ (Holm $p = 5.07 \times 10^{-5}$ vs. monolithic) with two active experts per tracing, reducing dynamic inference compute to 297.8 MFLOPs (a 28.2\% reduction relative to dense routing, though requiring $2.40\times$ the FLOPs of the 123.9 MFLOP monolithic baseline). Across training seeds, the minor numerical difference between dense routing and sparse Top-2 ($0.9118$ vs. $0.9113$, cross-seed paired $t$-test $p = 0.163$) is not statistically significant, though this descriptive proximity does not establish formal equivalence.

The router specializes in cardiac pathologies: Conduction Defects (CD) and Hypertrophy (HYP) predominantly activate TCN-1D and MultiScale-CNN ($w_k > 0.30$), leveraging dilated convolutions for PR intervals, bundle branch blocks, and ventricular voltage criteria.

Conversely, ResNet-1D and ConvAttention-1D exhibit dominant gating weights ($w_k > 0.28$) for localized ST-elevations and reciprocal depression in MI and STTC. Two lowest-relevance models are dynamically bypassed per patient, maintaining discrimination ($0.9113$) close to dense 4-expert routing ($0.9118$). Parameter budgets are matched within $\pm 3.0\%$ across arms, but inference compute is not: Top-2 routing uses 297.8 MFLOPs (latency $3.42 \pm 0.08$ ms on NVIDIA Quadro T1000) versus 123.9 MFLOPs ($1.99 \pm 0.05$ ms) for the monolithic baseline.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Evaluation of Formal Hypotheses}
\label{subsec:disc_hypotheses}

Under an approximately fixed parameter budget, capacity splitting acts as a variance-reduction regularizer: Multiplicity Superiority (H1) is validated as $K = 2, 4$ experts statistically outperform monolithic scale ($K = 1$, DeLong $p = 3.63 \times 10^{-4}$)~\cite{lakshminarayanan2017simple, chen2025budget}. For \textit{Architectural Diversity (H2)}, evaluating heterogeneous against homogeneous ensembles shows a consistent improvement trend (Holm $p = 0.058$) in discrimination and calibration (Brier 0.0867 vs 0.0881) by reducing CKA similarity (0.9008 vs 0.9458)~\cite{zhou2025diversity}, though it does not reach significance at $\alpha = 0.05$. In \textit{Sparse Adaptive Routing (H3)}, Top-2 routing achieves competitive discrimination ($0.9113 \pm 0.0008$ vs dense routing $0.9118 \pm 0.0004$; paired $p = 0.163$) while cutting FLOPs by 28.2\% relative to dense ensembling (297.8 vs 414.9 MFLOPs), without implying formal statistical equivalence. Under \textit{Selective Deferral (H4)}, combining predictive entropy and ensemble disagreement yields well-ordered uncertainty, achieving a $4.18\%$ relative risk reduction in retained Hamming error at 90\% coverage ($p = 0.0093$)~\cite{kompa2024second, argha2025selective}.

\subsection{Negative Results and Theoretical Boundaries}
\label{subsec:disc_negative}

\textbf{Capacity Under-Allocation and Gating Collapse:} At $K=16$ ($\sim$125K parameters/expert), sub-networks lack capacity for multi-lead extraction, tripling latency to 6.78~ms. Furthermore, training the router using diagnostic loss alone collapses into a single expert ($0.9049$ vs $0.9108$), demonstrating that diversity regularization is essential.

\subsection{Practical Deployment Implications and Limitations}
\label{subsec:disc_translation_limitations}

For resource-constrained devices, Top-2 sparse routing cuts inference FLOPs by $28.2\%$ relative to dense ensembling, while uncertainty deferral isolates ambiguous tracings for clinician review. These benchmarks demonstrate technical feasibility rather than clinical efficacy. \textbf{Limitations:} Assessments were conducted under edge parameter budgets ($\sim$2.0M parameters); evaluating foundation scales is left for future work~\cite{li2024ecgfounder, xu2026ecgmoe}. Parameter budgets are matched across arms, but compute is not: Top-2 requires 297.8 MFLOPs versus 123.9 for baseline. Crucially, the site-shift evaluation uses an internal held-out subset of PTB-XL ($s \ne 0$) and does not substitute for independent external multi-hospital validation.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This study demonstrates that fixed-budget model multiplicity statistically outperforms monolithic scale in 12-lead ECG diagnosis, while architectural diversity exhibits a consistent descriptive improvement trend ($p = 0.058$). Distributing a $\sim$2.0M parameter budget across compact specialized networks balances edge efficiency and diagnostic reliability.
